# Cute Character / 0.1.0 / experimental

Visual style, not a narrative genre or a promise of skeletal animation.
Set style_bible.preset_id to cute_character. Preserve the source's events and facts.
Use an original recurring character only where a person or guide serves the story;
never insert a fictional witness into a historical event or change narration.

Design: rounded silhouettes, two-to-three-head stylization when appropriate,
simple readable hands, dark plum continuous contour, matte flat colors, subtle paper
grain. Use pale cool backgrounds, jade and coral accents; avoid uniform beige wash.
Prefer broad graphic color blocks and sparse backgrounds over painterly anime detail.
Do not add companion pets or extra people unless present in the source. A character
depicted inside a monitor must not be duplicated as an animal in the surrounding room.
Choose one costume, facial proportion, hair silhouette and line-weight family.
Record them in character_rendering and continuity assets. Do not invent an approval.

Stage each cut around one action or observation. Establish the place, then show the
relevant reaction or evidence; do not repeat this order mechanically for every sentence.
Write a specific gaze target and expression for each relevant character cut.
Keep face, hands and important objects inside the camera crop at both endpoints.
Leave the caption safe area clear. Never bake text, arrows or UI labels into artwork.

For slide cinematic output, use deliberate still poses and restrained camera motion.
Do not request unsupported rigging, mouth animation or simulated interaction.
Use a new reviewed expression image for a changed emotion rather than stretching a face.
When continuity is off, flag cross-cut identity as unverified, not guaranteed.
Review a wide shot, reaction close-up and explanatory cut before claiming style readiness.

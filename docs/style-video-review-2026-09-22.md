## Cute character: REVISE

Positive: silhouette, bob haircut, cardigan, room geometry and major palette remain
recognizable across the neutral and smiling image states. The monitor communicates
the generated result clearly. Face remains in frame in sampled images.

Problem: image appearance and smile are part of the same whole-image transition.
It does not demonstrate stimulus, gaze acquisition and subsequent emotional change.
The character and loading symbol otherwise remain static. Do not label this as
rigged acting or promote REACTION_READY.

Next correction: preserve the approved room and character design; author independent
monitor-result and facial states, with reviewed anchors. Show the result before the
reaction. Check transition frames at full output rate, not just one-second samples.
Do not move rectangular crops of the illustration to imitate body articulation.

## Fantasy animation: REVISE

Positive: wide landscape establishes place and medium framing foregrounds the
traveler. Cape, clasp, tunic, hairstyle and satchel are broadly consistent. Restrained
camera movement is appropriate for a cinematic still sequence.

Problem: the narration says wind reveals the route, but the initial landscape already
shows a route and the images do not establish a distinct conceal/reveal event.
This is a semantic staging failure, not a missing decorative effect. Caption placement
also overlaps lower-body/path information. Small-screen readability needs a native
mobile playback check; reduced contact sheets cannot settle it.

Next correction: retain the source narration. Author before/after route visibility
with a consistent location, or separately controlled mist only after asset review.
Reserve a caption-safe composition area without moving focus away from the traveler
and route. Do not substitute a rewritten narration merely to pass the visual test.

## Candidate production rules (not universal film rules)

1. A narrated state change needs observable before/after evidence in its target.
2. For perception/reaction scenes, track stimulus and response as separate events.
3. Keep style quality, motion capability and technical delivery as separate verdicts.
4. Compare styles under a matched script before attributing quality differences to style.
5. Treat caption safety as a composition check, not merely a successful text render.

These findings are research evidence only. They do not automatically alter production
policies or replace the user's final approval.
